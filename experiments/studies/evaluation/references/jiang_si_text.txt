Supplementary Methods
Supplementary Methods S1. Sequence-cluster construction and threshold 
sensitivity
To assess the sensitivity of internal validation to sequence-based fold construction, 
GDPa1 antibodies were clustered using paired VH/VL sequence similarity. Antibodies 
connected above a specified similarity threshold were assigned to the same cluster, and 
clusters were then assigned to cross-validation folds. Thresholds of 0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 
0.95, and 0.98 were initially examined.

Thresholds of 0.80 and 0.85 generated dominant clusters that contained most antibodies 
and were therefore unsuitable for fold-based validation. Specifically, these thresholds 
produced highly imbalanced fold partitions and were excluded from the main 
comparison. Thresholds of 0.90, 0.95, and 0.98 were retained for threshold sensitivity 
analysis. Thresholds of 0.95 and 0.98 generated balanced fold partitions, whereas 0.90 
remained usable but less balanced because of one larger cluster.
Cluster-split performance was evaluated using the sequence-derived descriptor model 
and assay-wise Spearman correlation. The goal of this analysis was to examine the 
robustness of internal validation estimates to clustering threshold selection rather than 
to replace external held-out validation.
Supplementary Methods S2. GDPa3 8-assay sensitivity analysis
The main GDPa3 external validation analysis used seven directly matched assays 
shared between GDPa1 and GDPa3. As an additional sensitivity analysis, GDPa3 
titer_avg was mapped to the GDPa1 endpoint normalized_titer_productionbatch1_avg. 
Because the titer measurement scale and experimental context differed between the two 
datasets, this 8-assay analysis was not used as the primary external validation result. 
Instead, it was reported as a supplementary sensitivity analysis to evaluate whether 
inclusion of the mapped titer endpoint changed the overall interpretation of model 
transferability.
Supplementary Methods S3. PLAbDab application analysis
To assess the scalability of the scoring workflow as an application demonstration, 
paired antibody sequences were obtained from PLAbDab. Entries lacking either heavy- 
or light-chain sequences, containing non-standard amino acid characters, duplicated 
heavy–light chain pairs, or abnormal variable-region lengths were removed. Heavy-
chain sequences were restricted to 95–150 amino acids, and light-chain sequences were 
restricted to 85–130 amino acids to ensure compatibility with variable-region-based 
descriptor calculation. After filtering, 2,978 paired VH/VL antibodies were retained.
For each retained PLAbDab antibody, sequence-derived developability descriptors 
were calculated using the same descriptor workflow. Missing descriptor features 
required for scoring were imputed using statistics derived from the GDPa1 training 
descriptor table. The trained models were applied to generate predictions for the eight 
developability-related endpoints analyzed in GDPa1. Predicted assay values were 
converted into direction-aware desirability scores and further aggregated into domain-
level and overall developability scores.
Candidate antibodies were ranked by the overall developability score. In addition, a 
balanced prioritization strategy was applied by requiring a minimum assay-level 
desirability score of at least 0.30, thereby reducing the selection of candidates with 
strong liabilities in individual assays. Because PLAbDab lacks experimental assay 
labels matched to the GDPa1 endpoints, this analysis was considered a supplementary 
application demonstration rather than a strict external performance validation.
Supplementary Methods S4. Empirical nonspecificity liability filtering
As an optional supplementary application module, predicted developability scores were 

combined with an empirical nonspecificity liability filter based on published antibody 
developability principles. The filter flagged sequence- and structure-associated risk 
features, including enrichment of aromatic or positively charged residues in CDRs, 
arginine-rich paratopes, long CDRH3 regions, high Fv charge or pI, and exposed 
hydrophobic, aromatic, charged, or W/F/Y/R-rich surface patches. This rule-based 
module was used only as an application-oriented triage aid and was not included in 
model training, internal validation, or GDPa3 external validation.

and sequence-cluster splitting at thresholds of 0.90, 0.95, and 0.98. Spearman correlation was

from this table because they generated dominant clusters and invalid or highly imbalanced fold 
partitions, as shown in Table S5. Cluster range was calculated as the difference between the 
maximum and minimum Spearman correlations across the retained cluster thresholds. The mean 
row summarizes average assay-level Spearman correlation across the eight endpoints.

PLAbDab antibodies were ranked by predicted overall developability score in the supplementary 
developability-only application analysis. Minimum assay desirability indicates the lowest predicted 
desirability score among the eight GDPa1 assay endpoints, and limiting assay indicates the endpoint 
responsible for this minimum value. Assay liabilities < 0.30 reports the number of assay-level 
desirability scores below the balanced prioritization threshold. Passes balanced criterion indicates 
whether the antibody had no assay-level desirability score below 0.30. Because PLAbDab lacks 
experimental labels matched to the GDPa1 assay endpoints, this table should be interpreted as a 
supplementary computational triage result rather than experimentally validated candidate selection.
